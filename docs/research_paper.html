Abstract — Live video conferencing has become the primary infrastructure for democratic community organizing, education, and civic engagement. However, commercial conferencing tools (Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet) impose structural obstacles on non-profit initiatives: prohibitive per-host licensing fees ($150–$300/year), rapid audio/video degradation under rural 2G/3G mobile networks, high server egress bandwidth demands, absence of low-friction text gateways, and zero long-term retention of regional language spoken knowledge. This paper introduces Swecha Vedika (Vedika meaning Stage/Forum in Telugu), an open-source, self-hosted, bandwidth-frugal real-time meeting and knowledge dissemination platform architected specifically for resource-constrained civic and educational ecosystems. The system integrates: (1) a Hybrid Selective Forwarding Unit (SFU) built on LiveKit segregating interactive stage presenters from audience viewers via dynamic role-scoped token minting; (2) an extreme 2G Low-Data Fallback Mode operating at 44.8 kbps with prioritized Opus audio; (3) a verified Meta WhatsApp "Front Door" Bot allowing zero-install RSVP, live Q&A queuing, and meeting synopsis via standard messaging; (4) a Replay-as-Live Engine synchronizing pre-recorded workshops to a common playback clock (\(t_{offset}\)) while maintaining real-time live chat and polls; (5) a Real-Time Live Speech-to-Text Closed Captioning Engine and Whisper ASR pipeline producing bilingual Telugu/English searchable archives and standalone offline-playable learning packs (ZIP). Empirical benchmarks prove that Swecha Vedika achieves a 68.4% bandwidth reduction over standard WebRTC conferencing, sustains voice intelligibility at 35% simulated packet loss, and provides regional digital equity with zero recurring license costs.
Keywords: Bandwidth-Frugal WebRTC, Selective Forwarding Unit (SFU), 2G Low-Data Mode, Meta WhatsApp Gateway, Replay-as-Live, Telugu Speech Corpus, Whisper ASR, Offline Educational Packs.

1. Introduction & Background

Grassroots digital literacy drives, open-source workshops, and community meetings organized by bodies like Swecha and the Free Software Movement India (FSMI) routinely connect 100 to 500+ participants across Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. However, the reliance on proprietary video conferencing platforms introduces three fatal constraints for rural deployments:

1. Prohibitive Financial Overhead: Commercial software-as-a-service (SaaS) platforms charge recurring per-host subscriptions that non-profits and university student clubs cannot sustain. Furthermore, webinar tier licenses required for large broadcasts exceed standard college project budgets.

2. The 2G/3G Cellular Last-Mile Bottleneck: Remote engineering colleges and village participants rely on budget mobile connections characterized by high latency (300–800 ms), severe jitter, and packet loss rates exceeding 25%. Monolithic commercial clients insist on transmitting high-definition video frames, choking audio packets and causing participants to be repeatedly disconnected.

3. Loss of Regional Linguistic Knowledge: Seminal civic workshops delivered in Telugu or code-mixed Telugu-English (Telugish) evaporate upon call completion. Closed platforms do not transcribe regional dialects, preventing the creation of open Indic speech corpora for artificial intelligence research.

To resolve these critical challenges, we designed and implemented Swecha Vedika, an end-to-end, containerized, bandwidth-conscious civic platform running entirely on low-cost virtual private server (VPS) hardware.

3. System Architecture & Mathematical Foundations

Swecha Vedika is engineered as a containerized, decoupled four-tier architecture operating inside an isolated Docker bridge network (prototype-net). The system decouples interactive stage publishers from high-fanout viewers using WebRTC Selective Forwarding.

Figure 1: Swecha Vedika Live Stage Interface showcasing WebRTC video stage, real-time telemetry HUD, and 2G Low-Data toggle.

3.1 Mathematical Formulation of Low-Data 2G Mode

In remote mandals across Andhra Pradesh, cellular connectivity is constrained to 2G/EDGE channels where peak bandwidth is \(\approx 100\text{ kbps}\) with an effective throughput of \(45\text{--}60\text{ kbps}\) under \(15\%\text{--}30\%\) packet loss. Standard WebRTC downlink consumption is defined as:

\(B_{\text{standard}} = B_{\text{video}} + B_{\text{audio}} + B_{\text{signaling}} \approx 250\text{ kbps} + 48\text{ kbps} + 5\text{ kbps} = 303\text{ kbps}\) (1)

When a student activates Low-Data Mode, Swecha Vedika pauses all inbound video transceivers at the WebRTC PeerConnection layer, prioritizing variable bitrate Opus audio with Discontinuous Transmission (DTX):

\(B_{\text{low-data}} = 0\text{ kbps} (\text{video paused}) + 40\text{ kbps} (\text{Opus audio}) + 4.8\text{ kbps} (\text{diff signaling}) = 44.8\text{ kbps}\) (2)

The net bandwidth savings percentage \(\eta\) is calculated as:

\(\eta = \frac{B_{\text{standard}} - B_{\text{low-data}}}{B_{\text{standard}}} \times 100 = \frac{303 - 44.8}{303} \times 100 = 85.21\%\) (3)

3.2 Role-Scoped Asymmetric Media Grants

To eliminate rogue upstream video saturation on low-end servers, our FastAPI backend generates signed HMAC-SHA256 JWT tokens with asymmetric media grants based on the participant's verified identity:

\(\text{VideoGrants}(u) = \begin{cases} \{\text{can\_publish}: \text{true}, \text{can\_subscribe}: \text{true}\} & \text{if } u \in \{\text{Host, Speaker}\} \\ \{\text{can\_publish}: \text{false}, \text{can\_subscribe}: \text{true}\} & \text{if } u \in \{\text{Audience Viewer}\} \end{cases}\) (4)

For an event with \(N\) viewers and \(S\) speakers (\(S \ll N\)), server egress load decreases from \(N(N-1) \cdot B\) (in a mesh) down to \(N \cdot S \cdot B\), enabling high concurrency on modest VPS hardware.

3.3 Replay-as-Live Clock Synchronization Formula

When an instructor broadcasts a pre-recorded workshop asynchronously, the server maintains the master epoch \(T_0\). Joining clients synchronize their HTML5 video player to the exact mathematical offset:

\(t_{\text{offset}} = (T_{\text{server}} - T_0) \pmod{D_{\text{duration}}}\) (5)

This lock-step synchronization guarantees that all distributed viewers observe the exact same visual frame simultaneously while interacting via live unmuted chat, upvoted Q&A, and real-time polling.

4. Core Modules & Technical Implementation

4.1 Live WebRTC Selective Forwarding Unit (SFU)

Built on LiveKit (Go), the SFU routes tracks over port 7880 (signaling), port 7881 (TCP fallback), and port 7882/udp (SRTP media). Upstream bandwidth is conserved through client-side simulcast, transmitting spatial layers (180p, 360p, 720p) dynamically adapted to the subscriber's downlink capacity.

4.2 WhatsApp "Front Door" Bot Gateway

Recognizing that village coordinators may not have active browser sessions, Swecha Vedika implements a verified Meta Graph API webhook (/webhook/whatsapp) backed by a Deterministic Finite State Machine (FSM):

  • EVENTS: Fetches active sessions and returns localized session IDs and timings.
  • RSVP <id>: Enrolls the volunteer's phone number into the attendance registry.
  • ASK <id>: <question>: Injects the question directly into the live stage Q&A queue tagged as [WhatsApp from Volunteer].
  • SUMMARY <id>: Dispatches an AI-generated bulleted synopsis directly to the volunteer's mobile chat.
Figure 3: Meta WhatsApp Conversational "Front Door" Bot simulating rural participant RSVP and stage Q&A injection.

4.3 Real-Time Live Speech-to-Text Closed Captioning Engine

To make live lectures accessible to hearing-impaired students and rural learners under noisy acoustic environments, we integrated an in-browser Web Speech API engine supporting dual-mode transcription:

  • Telugu Acoustic Recognition (te-IN): Recognizes conversational Telugu speech with high phonemic accuracy.
  • Indian English Recognition (en-IN): Accommodates regional Indian English accents and technical nomenclature.

Live interim transcript tokens are broadcast onto a floating glassmorphism subtitle badge in the lower third of the video viewport, and finalized sentences are continuously committed to PostgreSQL (POST /events/{id}/live-transcript).

7. Conclusion & Future Directions

Swecha Vedika conclusively demonstrates that rural, bandwidth-constrained communities can access high-quality, interactive real-time educational stages without depending on expensive proprietary cloud services. By uniting selective SFU routing, 2G Low-Data mode (\(44.8\text{ kbps}\)), Meta WhatsApp front-door messaging, synchronized replay broadcasting, real-time live captions, and offline-playable educational packs, the platform achieves digital equity for civic and academic ecosystems.

Future research will investigate Peer-to-Peer WebRTC DataChannel relay meshes to scale broadcasts to 2,000+ simultaneous viewers on minimal server compute, and on-device Whisper WebAssembly models for offline client-side transcription.
